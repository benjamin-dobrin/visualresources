# Editing the website

You don't need to touch any code. Everything on the site that changes — projects,
photos, phone numbers, the map, the client list — is edited through a form at
**[app.pagescms.org](https://app.pagescms.org)**.

Sign in with GitHub, open the `visualresources` repository, and you'll see two things
in the sidebar: **Work** and **Site details**.

Every change you save is published automatically. The site rebuilds itself and the new
version is live in about a minute.

---

## Adding a project

**Work → Add an entry.** Fill in the form:

| Field | What to put |
|---|---|
| **Project title** | Short and plain: "Clubhouse corridor wraps", not "Corridor Wrap Project 2026" |
| **Client** | Their name, or leave `[Client]` if they haven't agreed to be named |
| **City / State** | Where it was installed |
| **Kind of work** | Pick the closest one. It's used as the label above the project title |
| **Spec line** | One line of facts, separated by `·` — see below |
| **Main photo** | The wide finished shot. Upload or pick from the library |
| **More photos** | Up to six detail and install shots |
| **Show on the homepage** | The homepage shows the first six featured projects |
| **Order** | Lower comes first. Use `1` for your strongest project |
| **The story** | Three sections: The brief, What we did, The result |

### The spec line

This is the small typewriter line under every photo, and it's the detail that makes the
site look like a producer made it. Facts only, separated by a middot:

```
96 × 48 in · ACM · 3M IJ180Cv3 · qty 42 · 14 states · installed Oct 11–12
20 × 30 ft island · SEG fabric, backlit · built NJ · installed day 0
310 linear ft · mesh + ACM · permit filed and approved
```

Leave out anything you don't know rather than guessing. No sentences, no adjectives.

### The story

Keep the three headings and write two or three sentences under each.

- **The brief** — who the client was, what they needed, and what made it hard. A deadline,
  an odd wall, landlord rules, matching a brand across sites.
- **What we did** — the design direction, the materials and why, who fabricated it, how
  install day went. This is where being one accountable producer shows.
- **The result** — the outcome in a sentence, plus a client quote if you have one.

Anything in square brackets like `[Client]` or `[Size]` is a placeholder waiting for a
real answer. Search the site for `[` before launch and clear them out.

## Photos

Photos matter more than words on this site. What works:

- **Wide, and in context.** The whole sign or wall, with the room or street around it.
- **Finished, and lit.** Daylight or dusk outside, lights on inside.
- **Something human for scale** where you can — a person walking through, a crew on a lift.
- **One process shot per project** is worth a lot: a proof, a crate, install day.

Avoid photos taken at an angle from across a room, anything with a hand or a phone in
frame, and screenshots of artwork. Upload the original from your phone or camera; the
site shrinks and converts them automatically, so a large file is fine.

## Site details

**Site details** in the sidebar holds everything that isn't a project.

- **Producer** — name, role, both phone numbers and the address. The two "for tapping to
  call" fields are the same numbers with no spaces and a `+1` in front, like
  `+14843518100`. They make the number tappable on a phone.
- **Rollout figures** — the three numbers in the rollouts section. They currently read
  `[—]`. Fill in real ones when you have them: locations installed, states.
- **Cities on the map** — every city with a red or black dot. To add one, click add,
  type the city and state, then search the web for "<city> latitude longitude" and paste
  the two numbers in. Tick "one of our bases" only for Philadelphia and New York.
- **Trusted by** — the client names across the top of the homepage. Only list clients who
  have said yes.
- **Quotes** — client testimonials. One shows on the homepage.
- **Homepage headline and photo** — the biggest thing a visitor sees. Change with care.

## Rules of thumb

1. **Never name a client who hasn't agreed.** Use `[Client]` or the industry instead.
2. **One new project a month** keeps the site alive and gives Google something new.
3. **Ask for a testimonial when you close a job**, while the client is happy.
4. **Don't invent numbers.** An empty figure is better than a wrong one.
5. **Look at it on your phone** after saving. Most visitors are on one.

## If something looks wrong

Every save is recorded and can be undone, so nothing is ever really broken. If a page
looks off after a change, note what you changed and ask for it to be reverted.

If you saved a change and the site hasn't updated after a few minutes, it's usually the
browser showing you an old copy. Hard refresh with `Cmd + Shift + R` (Mac) or
`Ctrl + Shift + R` (Windows).
